# Decide what counts as human-typed when choosing what to scan

Open question in `docs/design/README.md`. The audit's carried-over requirement
is to distinguish human-typed text from runtime-written text when deciding what
to scan, and the rule that does it has not been written.

The two ends are clear. `UserPromptSubmit` is a human typing; a tool result is
the runtime. The middle is not: a `Write` body the model composed out of a file
it had read is neither, and it is the case where a secret most plausibly
travels — read from one place, written to another, in context the whole time.

## Why the distinction is wanted at all

Scanning everything is the safe default and is also how a scanner becomes
noise: the same content gets flagged at every hop it makes. Scanning only
human-typed text catches the pasted credential and misses everything the model
moves.

A plausible resolution is to scan by **origin of the bytes** rather than by
hook, deduplicating on the content hash the findings already carry for dedup —
so a secret is reported at its first crossing and not at the four after it.
That is a proposal, not the answer; settle it here.
