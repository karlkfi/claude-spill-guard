# env into a process substitution is refused only by accident

`env > >(cat >&2) | cut -d= -f1` copies the whole dump to stderr and is refused.
It is refused because the segmenter records the first `>` as a redirect whose target is the word `>`, so `StdoutFile` is non-empty and `copiedAround` reads fd 1 as going to a file.
Nothing reads the `>(…)` body.
Found by the reviewer of #293 at `1baad9a`, reading the code; the refusal itself was driven there.

The grep path pins its twin, `| head > >(cat) |`, and no test pins the `env` spelling.
So a change to how `> >(` is lexed can turn this into an allow with every test green.

Two things to do, in order.
Add the arm to `TestPositionInThePipelineDecidesIt`, driven through the hook as the `<(cat >&2)` arms are.
Then decide whether a `>(…)` on the dump's own stage should be judged by `substitutedAround`, as a `<(…)` on a later stage is, so the refusal rests on the body rather than on a filename nobody wrote.

`copiedAround` and `substitutedAround` are in [`internal/hook/grep.go`](../../internal/hook/grep.go).
