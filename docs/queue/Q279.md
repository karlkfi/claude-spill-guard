# env into a process substitution is refused by way of a filename

`env > >(cat >&2) | cut -d= -f1` copies the whole dump to stderr and is refused.
`copiedAround` refuses a `>(` on purpose: its comment lists one among the names it cannot settle.
What is accidental is the route.
The segmenter records the first `>` as a redirect whose target is the word `>`, so `StdoutFile` is `>` and the refusal fires on that name; nothing reads the `>(…)` body.
Read off `bash.Segments` on `d0b776d`, where the hook denies the command under bash and zsh.

The grep path pins its twin: `grep -rn foo . > >(cat >&2) | spill-guard filter` is among the refused commands in `internal/hook/grep_test.go`.
No test pins the `env` spelling, so a change to how `> >(` is lexed can turn it into an allow with every test green.
Found by the reviewer of #293 at `1baad9a`.

## What to do

Add the arm to `TestPositionInThePipelineDecidesIt`, driven through the hook as the `<(cat >&2)` arms are.
That is the whole of the fix this row is sure of.

A second step is a choice, and it gives something up.
Judging the `>(…)` body with `substitutedAround`, as a `<(…)` on a later stage is judged, would rest the refusal on the body rather than on a filename nobody wrote.
On the `env` path a body is judged by its writes alone, so `env > >(sort -c) | cut -d= -f1` would go from a deny to an allow, and under bash `sort -c` names the first out-of-order `NAME=value` line on stderr.
The reviewer of #301 drove that.
It would match `env | sort -c | cut -d= -f1`, which `d0b776d` already allows, so it is consistent and it still trades away a refusal that works today.
Take the pin first and leave the second step until something needs it.

`copiedAround` and `substitutedAround` are in [`internal/hook/grep.go`](../../internal/hook/grep.go).
