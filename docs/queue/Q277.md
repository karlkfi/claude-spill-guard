# A refusal past a slow glob still defers when it is not env

Q270 settled one shape refusal ahead of the budget: an `env` dump is refused as soon as the walk reaches it, and one that opens no file does not wait on a glob elsewhere in the string.
The other two refusals this hook makes on a shape still wait.
Driven by the reviewer of #300 on a binary built from `8dbbf21`, with a glob over a home directory that takes longer than the 45-second budget to expand:

| command | outcome |
|---|---|
| `<home glob>; cat .env` | defers with a coverage record at 45.0s |
| `<home glob>; grep -r AKIA .` | defers with a coverage record at 45.0s |
| `env; <home glob>` | denied in 0.39s |

The first two behave as they did on `0fed400`, before Q270, so nothing regressed.
What is left is that a guarded-path read and a recursive grep are each a refusal that needs no file opened, and a slow operand ahead of them in the same command turns each into a deferral, which lets the call through.

`runPaced` in [`internal/hook/hook.go`](../../internal/hook/hook.go) and `walk` in [`internal/hook/deadline.go`](../../internal/hook/deadline.go) are where Q270 put the `env` decision.
`envDumped` runs ahead of the operand loop in [`internal/hook/bash.go`](../../internal/hook/bash.go); the guarded-path and recursive-grep decisions sit inside that loop, after the stat and the glob expansion.
So the question is one of ordering rather than of a decision that opens nothing: the grep refusal needs to know its own operand is a directory, which is a stat on that operand and not on the slow one ahead of it.
The next step is to find out whether each segment's refusal can be settled from its own operands before an earlier segment's glob is expanded.
If it can, pin each with an arm in the test Q270 added, `TestARefusalOnItsShapeDoesNotWaitOnTheBudget`, with the slow operand ahead of the refused one.

[Q132](Q132.md) is the row about no fixture reaching the budget, and [Q278](Q278.md) is the other half of what a cut walk loses.
