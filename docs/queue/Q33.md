# Measure what the card placeholder denylist misses

`NotPlaceholderCard` lists 34 numbers, the union of two gateways' primary
"valid test card" tables read 2026-08-22 — Stripe's 18 rows and Braintree's 19,
sharing three. That boundary was chosen because it is reproducible, not because
it is complete, and two shapes are known to be outside it.

**A gateway's behavioural matrix.** Stripe publishes 149 distinct numbers once
the decline, 3-D Secure, dispute and per-country tables are counted; 148 pass
Luhn. Those appear in the fixtures of anything integrating with Stripe, which
is exactly the material a scanner reads. Listing them is 115 more entries from
one vendor, and the next vendor starts the argument again.

**Sequential runs.** `1234567890123452` passes Luhn and is not a run of one
digit, so nothing drops it. A structural rule for ascending digits is the kind
of guess that suppresses a real card, which is what the denylist exists to
avoid.

So this is a measurement rather than a design question, and Q7 took the first
reading. `testdata/corpus/clean/payments.md` carries six numbers; five are on
the denylist and the sixth is `4000 0000 0000 0002`, Stripe's generic-decline
card, which passes Luhn, passes `NotPlaceholderCard`, and is dropped only
because no label sits within 64 bytes of it. That is one instance of the first
shape above and it is the shape the denylist cannot see.

What is left is the size of the residue. Widen the fixture to a real Stripe
integration's test file — the decline, 3-D Secure, dispute and per-country
tables, not the primary one — and read what survives `card-placeholder` with
the label gate removed, because the label gate is what is currently hiding the
answer. If the residue is a handful of numbers, extend the list and say where
the new boundary is. If it is the whole matrix, the answer is a different
mechanism and the corpus is what says so.

Nothing here justifies widening the list on a hunch: an entry that is not a
published test number suppresses a real card, and that failure is invisible.
The negative table in `internal/validate/card_test.go` is what holds the line.

**The residue is the matrix, and `payment-card` stayed off on it.** Karl chose to
enable this rule subject to a precision measurement, took the reading on 2026-11-03,
and kept it off. Over the nine public payment projects swept, 1,559 hits survive
`card-placeholder` with the label gate removed, and 963 with it in place: 354
distinct values in 318 of 49,611 files. The label gate cannot close that, because a
fixture carries the label a real card would. `active_merchant` alone is 946 hits in
308 of its 864 files, and Odoo's payment READMEs fire in 11 files. Three non-payment
files fire on the label `cc`: a fivethirtyeight census CSV and two in go-cmp. Eight
populations hold 395,516 files, 332 of them hit; the table and what it leaves out are
in `rules/README.md`. No hit was identified as a real card.

That answers the question above: 354 values is not a handful, so the list is not the
mechanism, and the corpus was never going to say so, reading 0 throughout. Enabling
this rule now waits on a different mechanism, and whichever one comes has to survive
`active_merchant`.
