# `jwt` is the rule the scan now waits on

Seven of the ten enabled rules run at the prefilter's hit positions since
[pipeline step 4](../design/README.md#pipeline). `jwt` does not, and it is the
one whose keyword real content carries most often: `eyJ` is the head of any
base64-encoded JSON object, which is a shape that turns up in lock files, JWKS
documents, CI configuration and anything embedding a token by example.

Measured 2026-09-04 over this repository's own text, 1,355,099 bytes in 172
files, one process, `internal/scan/bench_test.go`:

| | Whole ruleset | `jwt` alone |
|---|---|---|
| whole-buffer pass | 7.12 MB/s | 62 MB/s |
| as shipped | 32.09 MB/s | 62 MB/s, unchanged |

At 32.09 MB/s the whole set costs 31.2 ns/byte and `jwt` alone costs 16.1 of
them. It is over half of what is left.

## Why it is excluded, and why the obvious repair is not one

The loader refuses it on the last of the five conditions in
[`internal/rules/anchor.go`](../../internal/rules/anchor.go): the longest match
has to be bounded. `eyJ[A-Za-z0-9_-]{8,}\.` is not, so the engine's threads stay
alive for as long as the class matches and one attempt reads to the end of the
buffer. Driven on 64 KiB of `-eyJ` repeated — a hit every four bytes, every byte
in the class — running the rule at each hit took **29.8s** against 12.2ms for
one whole-buffer pass, and the ratio grew from 1,723x at 16 KiB to 2,451x at 64
KiB, which is the quadratic it looks like.

Windowing the attempt is the repair that suggests itself and it is unsound as
written. `FindSubmatchIndex` over `text[hit:hit+W]` cannot say whether the
engine still had live threads at the window edge, so "the match ended before the
edge" does not mean the full buffer would have produced the same match:
`\b(aab|a)` over the window `aa` reports `a` at offset 0 with a byte to spare
and the full text reports `aab`. Any window needs a conclusiveness test the
`regexp` API does not expose, or a bound derived from the pattern — which is
what the boundedness condition already is.

Three shapes worth weighing, none measured:

- **Bound the pattern instead of the loop.** `{8,}` becomes `{8,1000}` — RE2's
  cap — and the rule becomes bounded at 2,024 bytes. It changes what the rule
  matches, so it needs a corpus case proving nothing real is longer, and 2,024
  bytes of reach makes the budget in `internal/scan/scan.go` one hit per 2 KiB.
- **A second bounded pattern in front of the unbounded one**, matched at the
  hit to decide whether to run the real one there. Two patterns per rule is a
  schema change and a second thing to keep in step.
- **Leave it.** The scan is 4.5x faster than it was and the budget it has to
  fit in is 45 seconds; `jwt` is the difference between 32 MB/s and about 62.

## The term after it

`aws-access-key-id` gains the least of the seven — 3.1x, against 71.8x for
`github-fine-grained-pat` — and the reason is the prefilter rather than the
match loop. It carries five keywords and `containsKeyword` walks the buffer
once per keyword per case-head, so it makes ten passes at 181 MB/s where a rule
with one keyword makes two at 896. That is a different mechanism from this row
and a smaller one, and it only matters once `jwt` is dealt with. It is named
here rather than filed so a groom can split it out when it is worth doing.
