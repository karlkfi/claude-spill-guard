# A scheduled prerelease rehearsal, once one has been priced

**Event:** the next prerelease rehearsal somebody runs by hand, with what it
cost written down — wall clock, how many tags and drafts it left behind, and
whether anything read them.

The deferred half of the row that added the signature negative control. That
row asked which of two things to build; the answer, recorded 2026-09-07, was
the negative control first and this one re-asked once a rehearsal has priced
its standing cost.

## What is now covered, and what is not

`scripts/check-signature-identity.py` verifies `checksums.txt` and then
requires three identities the release was not signed under to be rejected, and
`published-release-verify` runs that file on every pull request against the
last published release. So the cosign identity check is exercised without a
tag, and a `--certificate-identity` that stopped being enforced is caught.

Everything else behind the tag is not. The `release` job carries ten named
steps and the ones no pull request reaches are still the attestation minting,
the bundle upload and its empty-bundle guard, the byte-compare of published
assets against `dist/`, the published-body comparison, and the plugin-manifest
version check at its `${GITHUB_REF_NAME#v}` call site. A rehearsal runs all of
them for real. A negative control proves a verifier discriminates and cannot
prove a pipeline works.

## The measurement that argues for it

v0.1.0 was cut on 2026-09-02 and the runbook's mandatory rehearsal found four
defects, one per attempt, every one behind the tag:

| Attempt | What was wrong |
|---|---|
| `v0.1.0-rc.1` | `.goreleaser.yaml`'s `signs:` passed `--output-signature` and `--output-certificate`. cosign v3 defaults `--new-bundle-format` to true, which makes both no-ops and `--bundle` mandatory, so signing died with `create bundle file: open : no such file or directory`. |
| `v0.1.0-rc.1` again | `check-release-artifacts.py --signed` still required `checksums.txt.sig` and `.pem`, which the fix above stopped producing. |
| `v0.1.0` | `changelog: disable: true` makes GoReleaser ignore `--release-notes`. The draft published ten correct, signed, verifiable assets and a body of two newlines. |
| after publishing | Two arms of `release-claims-mutation-control` read real release state rather than forcing it, so publishing disarmed them. |

Three cost a tag cycle each. Only the first is the class the negative control
now reaches, and it reaches it in the flag-no-longer-enforced direction rather
than the signing-step-fails one.

## What the maintainer is being asked, and why it is not free

A schedule creates and deletes tags and draft releases on a public repository
on a timer, with nobody reading them. That is a publish outside the tree and a
standing cost this repository has not priced — which is the whole of why the
question was deferred rather than answered. The numbers a rehearsal should
bring back are how long the pipeline takes end to end, how many Rekor entries
and attestations it mints, and whether a deleted prerelease draft leaves
anything a scanner or a package channel can still see.

`docs/development/release-process.md` already tells a maintainer to rehearse by
hand under *Rehearsing without spending a version number*, and that section is
where the measurement comes from. It also carries the warning worth re-reading
before pricing this: three v0.1.0 rehearsals went green and none of them opened
the release body, because nothing said to.
