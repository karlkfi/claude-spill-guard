# A walk cut at the budget drops the buffers it had already collected

`cat <key file>; <home glob>` defers with a coverage record at 45.03s, where the glob takes longer than the budget to expand.
The key file is named first, resolves at once, and holds a planted key the shipped rules match.
The walk is cut at the glob before anything it had collected is scanned, so the finding in the first file is never reached and the call goes through.

Driven by the reviewer of #300 on a merge of `85b6619` with `afe4a7e`, and the same on `0fed400`, so Q270 did not cause it.
`CLAUDE.md` says a gap does not hide a finding beside it, and that a scan past its budget blocks on the buffers it got through.
Both are about the scan; this is the walk ahead of it running out, and the buffers it had in hand are discarded with it.

The remedy is not measured.
The reading to check first is whether `walk` in [`internal/hook/deadline.go`](../../internal/hook/deadline.go) can hand back the operands it resolved before the deadline, so the scan runs over those and the unresolved rest is recorded as the gap.
What bounds it is the hook's 60-second timeout: a scan started after a 45-second walk has to fit in what is left, or the hook is killed and writes nothing.

[Q277](Q277.md) is the refusal half of the same cut, and [Q276](Q276.md) is the floor the walk is given.
