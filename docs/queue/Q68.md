# Put a measurement next to the claim it licenses

Three separate sessions narrowed a finding they already held, on one pull
request, within two hours. Each narrowing was caught the same way, and it was
not by a check anybody ran.

## What happened

Working Q45, a claim about `stale-citation` was written and corrected three
times, each correction narrower than the evidence already in hand:

- The promotion was justified as "a merge cannot turn a correct citation
  stale" — false for every one of the class's four checks.
- The replacement sorted the checks into branch-decidable and merge-decidable —
  also false, and the row's own measured table two paragraphs above already
  carried the counter-example.
- A reviewer re-measured one check at a new head and reported "two arms, not
  one", having measured all four at the previous head and reported four.

The third is the clearest, because that session diagnosed it themselves: a
re-measure at a new head is scoped to the text under review, and a finding
already in hand does not get re-run, because it is not what changed.

## What caught it, each time

A conclusion and its own evidence visible on one screen. Q67's two tables
disagreed. The workflow's comment block contradicted the measured table four
lines above it. Q30 said the trigger had fired at line 109 and had not at line
132.

None of those needed a checker. They needed the two things to be adjacent, so
that reading one put the other in view.

## The ask

A convention line in [`README.md`](README.md): a row that draws a conclusion
from a measurement puts them next to each other, conclusion after evidence, in
the same section. Where a row states a conclusion far from its measurement,
that distance is the defect worth fixing before the prose is polished.

Small, and worth writing down because the failure mode is invisible from
inside — every one of the three sessions was reading carefully, and none of
them was reading the paragraph that contradicted them.

## Adjacency is necessary and not sufficient, measured

A later batch — six items, six pull requests, 2026-08-27 — produced three more
instances of a narrowed or false claim, and **one of them refutes the fix above
on its own terms.**

`rules/README.md` quoted a clause-free tree-wide count of 19. A later commit
*of the same branch* took the real figure to 21, by adding two PEM headers to a
test file. The stale number was sitting in the paragraph whose entire argument
is that this number drifts — conclusion and evidence already adjacent, in the
same sentence, and it went stale anyway.

So there is a second axis, and adjacency does not cover it. The first is
**spatial**: a conclusion far from its evidence, which is what this row was
filed about. The second is **temporal**: a claim that was true when written and
was falsified by a later commit in the same branch. Nothing about where the
sentence sits prevents that, because at the moment of writing the sentence was
correct and its evidence was right beside it.

The other two from that batch are the same shape:

- `docs/queue/Q76.md` asserted that a candidate regex "has not been driven
  against the clean half at all", eighteen lines below a reading, added two
  commits earlier, saying both forms report zero.
- A reviewer's coverage claim — that CI had exercised the head against current
  `main` — was true when written and false forty-eight seconds later, when the
  next pull request merged.

## What catches the temporal half

Not adjacency, and not a gate either — the numbers are real measurements, so
nothing static can tell 19 from 21 without re-running the measurement.

What did catch it, twice: **re-reading the branch's own prose against the final
tree before calling it ready**, as a distinct step from writing it. The author
of that pull request did exactly that at the end and corrected three claims —
a commit count, the 19, and a workflow citation to a SHA that a rebase had
removed. Two of the three had been correct when written.

So this row's ask grows one line: a row or a pull request body that quotes a
measured figure gets its figures re-derived against the final tree, not the
tree they were measured on. The distance between them is the branch's own
history, and it is invisible from inside any single commit.

## What this is not

Not a gate. Adjacency is not machine-checkable, and a checker for it would
be a worse instrument than a reader with both paragraphs on the screen. The
value is in writing rows that way, not in failing the ones that are not.

Not a claim that review is unnecessary — every one of these was caught by a
reviewer. It is a claim that the layout is what made them catchable.
