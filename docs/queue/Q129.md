# A line-number citation can point at the wrong site and pass

`queue.py lint` checks that a citation's fragment appears **at the line it
names**. Where the fragment is unique in the file that is a real check. Where it
is not, every occurrence satisfies it equally, so a pointer aimed at the wrong
one is green and says nothing.

`internal/hook/hook.go` carries `reading the file this call would send` twice —
the `os.Stat` arm and the `os.ReadFile` arm of `toolTargets`, thirty lines
apart in one function. [Q89](Q89.md) cites the first. Nothing enforces that, and
nothing would report a repoint onto the second.

## What it cost, measured on 2026-09-04

Four pull requests over one afternoon, three of which moved a cited file. Three
citation values came out where **both sides of a merge conflict were wrong**:

| Citation | Side A | Side B | Correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Q108.md` → `CLAUDE.md` | 336 (trunk) | 347 (branch) | **363** |
| `Q89.md` → `hook.go` | 414 (trunk) | 403 (branch) | **424** |
| `Q108.md` → `CLAUDE.md`, earlier round | 324 | 336 | **347** |

A resolver doing the reasonable thing — taking one side of a conflict it can
see — gets every one of those wrong. Only extracting the merged tree and reading
the line gives the answer, and that is not what a conflict marker invites.

**The `hook.go` row is the one this item is about.** On the trunk at that
moment, line 414 was a **closing brace**. A repoint onto it would have failed
the lint. Line 454 was the *other* occurrence of the same fragment, and a
repoint onto that would have **passed** — pointing at the `os.ReadFile` arm in
a row whose sentence is about the `os.Stat` one. Three sessions measured this
citation independently within an hour and returned three different numbers, each
correct for its own tree; two of the three write-ups were wrong about *which
arm* while being right about the number.

## Why this is not Q30, Q64, Q67 or Q99

Those four are all about the **window** — how far a fragment may have drifted
from the line named before the lint objects. [Q30](Q30.md) pins it,
[Q99](Q99.md) covers the drift a pull request causes itself, [Q64](Q64.md)
covers a path that cannot be cited at all, [Q67](Q67.md) covers a pointer
describing the merged tree.

This one is about the **check**, and it survives every window including zero:
`--citation-window 0` passes a wrong-site pointer, because the fragment really
is at the named line. Narrowing the window cannot reach it.

## What would close it

A reading first, because the cheap fix and the correct one differ.

**The cheap fix** is for the lint to refuse a citation whose fragment is not
unique in the file, which turns a silent wrong answer into a loud one and costs
nothing to implement. It also fires on every legitimate citation of a repeated
string, so the first measurement is how many of those the store has today.

**The better fix may be to stop citing lines.** A fragment plus a
distinguishing anchor — the enclosing function, or the preceding call — is
checkable, survives a merge, and cannot be aimed at the wrong site because it
names the site rather than a coordinate. Q89's own prose already resorts to
this: it says *the `os.Stat` arm*, because the number could not carry it.

Whichever wins, the exposure this is filed against is closed for now by
accident rather than by design: `docs/queue/Q108.md` was deleted with
[Q108](https://github.com/karlkfi/claude-spill-guard/pull/99), and no row in
the store cites a `CLAUDE.md` line any more. [Q89](Q89.md)'s pointer at line
424 of `internal/hook/hook.go` is the last line-number citation standing, in
the row whose subject is pointers going quietly stale.

That sentence had to be written without the `file:line` form, because the lint
reads any such shape as a citation of this row's own and refuses it — which is
this item demonstrating itself on the way in.

## Why it matters more than a PR-local mistake

`queue` promotes `stale-citation` on a push to `main` and holds it back on a
pull request. So a citation that is wrong on a branch is caught at review, and
one that is wrong **in the merged tree** reddens the trunk. All three values
above were resolved during a rebase, which is exactly where the merged-tree
value is the only correct one and the branch-local check cannot see it.
